Main framework

Boundaries of the Niche: Exergy, Institutional Claims, and Biological Continuity in Coupled Human Systems

Henrik Hagnell · Draft · 15 November 2026 · Main framework

Abstract

This draft develops a coupled state-space framework for reconstructing the material, institutional and biological conditions of human systems. It separates physical exergy reserves and persistent infrastructure from institutional rules, claims and models, while treating populations as adaptive biological systems with distinct response and developmental timescales. The framework examines how buffer depletion, institutional lag, demographic adjustment and cross-layer feedback affect the accessibility of incumbent and successor configurations. Scenario reconstruction is conditional on observable constraints, rather than a claim of inevitable collapse or a uniquely prescribed successor regime.

Keywords: dissipative structures; niche construction; exergy; institutional claims; biological continuity; regime transitions.

Relationship to framework: Parent framework defining x(t) = (b(t), m(t), a(t), u(t)) and the physical and temporal boundary conditions used by the companions.

Observable mechanism or hypothesis: Buffer coverage and refill rates, infrastructure maintenance balances, claims-versus-delivery discrepancies, demographic responses and changes in feasible regime configurations.

Companion research papers

The Vertical Excursion: How Human Institutions Emerged, Decoupled from Biology, and Built the Modern Macro-Basin

Henrik Hagnell · 15 November 2026 · Companion research manuscript

Abstract

This historical and evolutionary companion asks how resource concentration, defensible bottlenecks, durable storage, externalised records and repeated coordination demands selected for permanent vertical offices. It distinguishes temporary human hierarchies from institutions able to retain claims and operational memory beyond their current occupants. The analysis follows the interaction between vertical coordination, horizontal kin networks, physical maintenance costs and developmental adaptation, supplying the historical boundary conditions for the parent framework's institutional state-space.

Keywords: institutional evolution; resource circumscription; storage; transgenerational office; kinship networks; institutional persistence.

Relationship to framework: Pre-framework genesis module; supplies initial historical conditions and interpretive links to Chapters 1–2 and later institutional, demographic and trust mechanisms.

Observable mechanism or hypothesis: Persistence of offices across succession, storage and resource chokepoints, exit costs, mobilisation overhead and the balance between central maintenance and local networks.

Companion Module: Excitable Phase-Synchronisation, Neuroendocrine Calibration, and Multiplex Triage Cascades

Henrik Hagnell · 15 November 2026 · Companion research manuscript

Abstract

This modelling companion connects episodic biological stress and organisational commitment dynamics to nonlinear phase transitions. It distinguishes correlated exposure, threshold cascades and genuine oscillator synchronisation, then uses excitable active-rotator dynamics, common forcing, network coupling and inertia to describe possible aggregate signalling and hysteresis. Its proposed cross-scale correspondence is explicitly a testable mapping rather than an empirically established macro-social law; the paper identifies observable signatures needed to discriminate the mechanisms.

Keywords: active rotators; phase synchronisation; critical transitions; common noise; hysteresis; neuroendocrine calibration; multiplex networks.

Relationship to framework: Mathematical and micro-dynamical extension to framework Chapters 9–12, cross-linked to the Social Trust Sensor.

Observable mechanism or hypothesis: Phase-locking and cross-spectral coherence versus mere shared exposure; variance, phase-cluster splitting, threshold cascades and asymmetric recovery after common shocks.

Social Trust as an Observational Sensor for Institutional Niche Dynamics

Henrik Hagnell · Revised manuscript · 15 November 2026 · Companion research manuscript

Abstract

This paper formalises trust as a domain-specific, directional observational sensor operating on three response times: autonomic detection, pragmatic reliance and verification, and inherited declarative schemas. It preserves the parent DST–NCT state-space without treating trust as a fifth fundamental state or an exergy stock. A history-dependent observation operator links these clocks to exposure, care provision and institutional information. Proposed telemetry and falsification conditions test whether discordance among physiological response, demonstrated reliance and declared confidence improves detection of hidden institutional changes beyond surveys and macroeconomic accounts.

Keywords: relational trust; allostasis; generalised unsafety; information asymmetry; institutional resilience; hysteresis; care networks; resource buffering.

Relationship to framework: Three-clock empirical observation companion to framework Chapters 9–12, paired with the Excitable Phase-Synchronisation module.

Observable mechanism or hypothesis: Divergence between autonomic strain, practical checking or withdrawal, and declared trust, stratified by exposure, counterparty, care domain and network position.

Sub-Floor Firewalls: Binary Taboos, Verification Overhead, and Horizontal Substrate Damage

Henrik Hagnell · 22 November 2026 · Companion research manuscript

Abstract

This companion distinguishes three boundaries in the DST–NCT framework: the incumbent institutional separatrix (Σ), the care boundary (Σcare), and a deeper horizontal biological-social floor. It proposes that shared prohibitions around bodily non-predation, developmental safety, care of dependent people, physical-substrate integrity, and treatment of the dead reduce verification overhead in conditions of asymmetric vulnerability. The paper contrasts scarcity-driven breaches with administrative exception drift, drawing on the Chinese Great Famine and Canadian medical assistance in dying as comparative cases. Its central hypothesis is that some boundary violations impair cooperative trust and successor-niche formation independently of the survival or failure of the incumbent institution.

Keywords: anthropological boundaries; verification overhead; horizontal trust; care boundary; binary norms; institutional succession; hysteresis.

Relationship to framework: Tier II companion to the parent framework's distinction between institutional separatrices (Σ), the care boundary (Σcare) and biological continuity; linked especially to Chapters 4 and 11–12, Social Trust Sensor, and Excitable Phase-Synchronisation.

Observable mechanism or hypothesis: Assess whether introducing discretionary exceptions increases verification labour, disputed eligibility, defensive checking and long-horizon withdrawal, and whether boundary breaches predict persistent horizontal trust deficits after institutional reorganisation.

Narratives, Forward Strategy, and Institutional Observation

Henrik Hagnell · 15 November 2026 · Companion research manuscript

Abstract

This analytical guide distinguishes shared narratives that regulate expectations from forward strategy that commits resources under physical constraints. It traces how institutional continuity language can remain coherent while material delivery or maintenance changes, and proposes operational tests based on costly commitments, timing and realised capacity rather than rhetoric alone. The guide positions narratives as situated observation and coordination processes rather than independent material resources.

Keywords: institutional narratives; strategic commitments; schema mimicry; information asymmetry; physical investment; observation operators.

Relationship to framework: Epistemic and operational mapping linked to framework Chapters 2, 4 and 14 and to the trust and cultural companions.

Observable mechanism or hypothesis: Verifiable resource reallocations, completion deadlines, infrastructure service outcomes and differences between stated continuity and realised physical capacity.

Standalone Mapping: Shared Meaning-Systems, Religions, and Secular Faiths Across the Niche Lifecycle

Henrik Hagnell · 15 November 2026 · Companion research manuscript

Abstract

This mapping treats religious and secular meaning-systems as composite coordination architectures rather than unitary ideological entities. It identifies narratives, internalised behavioural rules, collective ritual, costly credibility signals and material care institutions, together with structural constraints on specialised roles. Their differing adjustment timescales explain how a seemingly unified institutional belief system can retain some functions while other components reorganise under material pressure.

Keywords: cultural evolution; religion; secular belief systems; ritual; common knowledge; care networks; institutional continuity.

Relationship to framework: Cultural and coordination mapping of framework Chapters 4 and 9–12; connects to Social Trust Sensor and Narrative Mapping.

Observable mechanism or hypothesis: Changes in ritual participation, costly commitments, care delivery, institutional credibility, intergenerational coordination and the persistence or modular recombination of component functions.

Cultural Simulation, Counterfactual Coordination, and Successor-State Telemetry

Henrik Hagnell · 15 November 2026 · Companion research manuscript

Abstract

This companion examines fictional worlds, games, reenactment and other cultural simulations as possible low-cost environments for exploring counterfactual social and resource arrangements. It distinguishes rehearsal that transfers into physical skills or coordination from symbolic substitution without such transfer. A six-domain observation matrix separates represented institutional rules, threat contingencies, cohort participation, network topology, conversion into action and production or patronage structures. Cultural signals remain subordinate to independently measured physical and demographic conditions.

Keywords: cultural simulation; counterfactual search; successor states; collective coordination; behavioural rehearsal; observation.

Relationship to framework: External cultural observation module for framework Chapters 4, 10, 12 and 14, cross-referenced to the Social Trust Sensor.

Observable mechanism or hypothesis: Movement from simulated rules and shared symbols to acquired physical competence, measurable preparation, coalition topology or committed resources.

Exogenous Canopy–Substrate Decoupling: Total War, Siege, Counterinsurgency, and Hybrid Subversion in the DST–NCT Framework

Henrik Hagnell · 15 November 2026 · Companion research manuscript

Abstract

This strategic companion applies the DST–NCT state-space to external physical and informational perturbations during total war, siege, counterinsurgency and economic coercion. It distinguishes attacks that reduce operational capacity from coercion intended to induce attitudes or political compliance, and describes how rationing monopolies and local protection can preserve dependence on an incumbent apparatus despite severe damage. Comparative conflict topologies are used to specify conditions under which physical degradation, behavioural decoupling and institutional replacement diverge.

Keywords: war; siege; counterinsurgency; civilian infrastructure; coercion; resource denial; institutional continuity.

Relationship to framework: Applied conflict module extending framework Chapters 13–14 and drawing upon trust, phase-synchronisation and cultural mappings.

Observable mechanism or hypothesis: Infrastructure repair versus recurrent disruption, delivered essentials, supply-route control, local protection, behavioural alignment and the existence of viable substitute arrangements.

Independent research

Distributed Wearable Autonomic Telemetry as a Macro-Social Sensor Array: Evolutionary Calibration, Spatio-Temporal Phase Synchronisation, and Institutional Observability

Henrik Hagnell · Standalone research and architecture paper · 18 November 2026 · Standalone research and architecture paper

Abstract

This standalone research and architecture paper investigates whether longitudinally calibrated consumer wearables can form a distributed macro-social sensor array. It combines wearable photoplethysmography and heart-rate variability, movement, temperature and sleep data with within-person calibration, geospatial aggregation and network-level synchronisation measures. It differentiates heterogeneous reactivity and recovery profiles while examining non-random device dropout, common-cause confounding, mathematical non-invertibility, network dependence and measurement gaming. The proposal is a research architecture and set of testable hypotheses, not a validated population-scale early-warning instrument.

Keywords: wearable sensing; heart-rate variability; photoplethysmography; population telemetry; spatio-temporal synchronisation; institutional observability; signal processing.

Relationship to framework: Independent paper, not a formal extension of the parent framework; it cites the Social Trust Sensor and Excitable Phase-Synchronisation companions as related mechanisms.

Observable mechanism or hypothesis: Within-person autonomic recovery, correlated residuals after movement and illness filtering, geographic phase coherence, slow recovery and representation or attrition bias.
