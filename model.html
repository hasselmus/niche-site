The model

The Niche model is a framework for reconstructing scenarios in coupled human systems. It traces resource constraints, institutional coordination and population responses on different timescales. Scenarios are conditional on their assumptions, not predictions of an inevitable outcome.

Three coupled layers

  • Material substrate. External driving conditions, usable exergy, stored reserves and persistent structures determine what activities can be supported and for how long. Material stocks and capacities change through use, construction, maintenance and deterioration.
  • Institutional coordinate system. Inherited rules, financial claims, procedures and models coordinate activity and allocate resources. These representations can adjust faster than the physical capacities on which they depend.
  • Biological response. Populations adjust labour, care, development, reproduction and planning horizons to their material and institutional conditions. Their continuity need not depend on the continuity of any particular institutional regime.

How the layers interact

Biological activity b(t) modifies the material niche m(t); the resulting conditions alter biological and institutional activity. Institutional arrangements a(t) organise and represent those interactions, while external drivers u(t) set boundary conditions. Differences in replenishment, response and adjustment timescales can produce persistence, depletion or transitions between regimes.

The model therefore assesses the viability of an incumbent regime N₀ separately from the viability of populations and possible successor regimes Nᵢ. The simulator explores selected couplings and boundary conditions.
