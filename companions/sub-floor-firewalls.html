Sub-Floor Firewalls: Binary Taboos, Verification Overhead, and Horizontal Substrate Damage

Research papers and citations

Henrik Hagnell · 22 November 2026 · Companion to Boundaries of the Niche (parent framework DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.24242901)

Abstract

This companion distinguishes three boundaries in the DST–NCT framework: the incumbent institutional separatrix (Σ), the care boundary (Σcare), and a deeper horizontal biological-social floor. It proposes that shared prohibitions around bodily non-predation, developmental safety, care of dependent people, physical-substrate integrity, and treatment of the dead reduce verification overhead in conditions of asymmetric vulnerability. The paper contrasts scarcity-driven breaches with administrative exception drift, drawing on the Chinese Great Famine and Canadian medical assistance in dying as comparative cases. Its central hypothesis is that some boundary violations impair cooperative trust and successor-niche formation independently of the survival or failure of the incumbent institution.

Catalogue summary based on the companion manuscript; not presented as its verbatim abstract.

Keywords: anthropological boundaries; verification overhead; horizontal trust; care boundary; binary norms; institutional succession; hysteresis.

Relationship to main framework: Tier II companion to the parent framework's distinction between institutional separatrices (Σ), the care boundary (Σcare) and biological continuity; linked especially to Chapters 4 and 11–12, Social Trust Sensor, and Excitable Phase-Synchronisation.

Observable mechanism or hypothesis: Assess whether introducing discretionary exceptions increases verification labour, disputed eligibility, defensive checking and long-horizon withdrawal, and whether boundary breaches predict persistent horizontal trust deficits after institutional reorganisation.
