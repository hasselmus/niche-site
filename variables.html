Variables and notation

Symbols used in the framework, grouped by function. Rates, stocks, timescales and institutional claims are kept distinct; most components require application-specific units and definitions.

Based on the framework draft of 21 October 2026. Chapter references indicate where the notation is defined or used. Symbols in the standalone simulator are not assumed to have identical implementations.

Coupled state-space · Thermodynamic and material balances · Characteristic times and horizons · Regimes and thresholds · Scenario reconstruction and observation

Coupled state-space

Chs. 1–2, 4, 5, 13

x(t)
Complete coupled state, written (b(t), m(t), a(t), u(t)). A bookkeeping tuple, not four interchangeable quantities.
b(t)
Biological state vector: activity, population, demographic structure and labour. In the human application it also contains inherited, developmental and physiological variation.
m(t)
Material and constructed-environment state vector: physical reserves, habitat, infrastructures and functional structures.
a(t)
Inherited state vector. The general biological model uses inherited traits or allele distributions; the human institutional mapping uses rules, claims, organisational practices and models.
u(t)
External driving conditions and physical boundary inputs, such as solar, climatic and geophysical forcing. Not governed by the institution in the chosen model boundary.
Fb, Fm, Fa
System-specific rate functions governing changes in b, m and a. Their signs and functional forms must be specified for the application.
arules(t)
Rules and operational procedures contained within the institutional state a(t).
aclaims(t)
Institutionally recognised claims and liabilities concerning present or future command over activity and resources.
amodels(t)
Institutional representations, forecasts, measurements and expectations used to coordinate activity.
xk(t)
The state of a specific bounded configuration or party k in the conflict/disaster application; consists of its own b, m, a and u.
btarget(t)
Population state of a specified target group when modelling substrate-directed violence.

Thermodynamic and material balances

Chs. 1 and 3

t
Elapsed time, shared across the coupled equations.
S
Entropy of a declared system, in joules per kelvin (J/K).
ΦS
Net entropy transfer rate into the system; may be negative, in J/(K·s).
σ
Internal entropy production rate; non-negative, in J/(K·s).
B(t)
Selected store of usable exergy, in joules (J), defined relative to a reference environment. It is not financial wealth.
Pin
Exergy transfer rate into the selected store B, in watts (W).
Pout
Exergy transfer rate out of B, in watts (W).
Pdest
Exergy destruction within the selected store, in watts (W).
s(t)
Vector of persistent constructed material states or service capacities within m(t); not a store of recoverable construction energy.
si(t)
One selected structural state, e.g. transport capacity, insulation performance or storage protection.
Gs
Construction or replacement contribution to structural capacity, expressed as a rate of change of the chosen structural state.
Rs
Maintenance and restoration contribution to structural capacity, in the same units as Gs.
Ds
Deterioration of structural function or capacity, in the same units as Gs.
Pconv
Exergy throughput supplied to construction or structural conversion.
Pmaint
Exergy throughput supplied to maintenance.

Characteristic times and horizons

Chs. 1–3 and 9

τb
Duration of a specified biological response or turnover process.
τm
Duration of change in a selected material store, habitat property or structure.
τa
In the core biological model, duration of inherited-trait or allele-frequency change; do not automatically equate it with institutional adjustment time.
τu
Duration of change in external forcing.
τfin
Fast symbolic and operational timescale: transactions, claims, algorithmic coordination and operational feedback.
τinst
Timescale for fundamental change in institutional rules and inherited operational structure.
τdev
Developmental calibration interval during which organisms sample conditions and form physiological, cognitive and reproductive responses.
τsig
Timescale for dispersed biological and behavioural responses to become a visible demographic signal.
H
Institutional representational horizon: how far forward a configuration assumes material and external continuity for present commitments.
Tcover
Duration a usable reserve can meet a defined shortfall; for a constant deficit, Bavailable/Pshortfall, provided delivery power is sufficient.
Taccum
Duration over which exergy for a subsequent pulse accumulates.
Tpulse
Duration over which concentrated mobilisation is discharged.
Trefill
Time required to replenish a reserve following a specified withdrawal.
Trecur
Interval between comparable withdrawals, pulses or mobilisation events.
Tconstruct
Construction or restoration time for a selected structural function.
Tservice
Duration for which a structure remains above a stated service threshold under specified use and maintenance.

Regimes and thresholds

Chs. 4, 11–13

N0
Incumbent institutional basin: the regime whose rules, material flows and population coordination are under examination.
Ni
A candidate successor regime, assessed under its own conditions rather than as a degraded version of N0.
Σ
Effective basin boundary or separatrix between dynamically distinct regimes.
Σcare
Care boundary: proposed social-biological threshold associated with common recognition of non-fulfilment of institutional care commitments.
Ni,exit
Successor pathway formed through outward withdrawal from the incumbent configuration (Chapter 12).
Ni,capture
Successor pathway formed by in-situ capture and separation of material and institutional modules from the incumbent centre (Chapter 12).

Scenario reconstruction and observation

Ch. 14

t0
Declared analytical present at which the inherited state is reconstructed.
c
Evidence cut-off: the latest information date admitted in a reconstruction.
yj
Reported observation j; not identical to the complete underlying state.
Oj
Observation operator mapping the bounded state and an observer’s coordinates to yj.
Rj
Spatial, population or organisational boundary of observation j.
Wj
Observation or measurement interval relevant to j.
pj
Information position of observer j.
Hj
Future horizon invoked by an observation or forecast, if any.
θj
Observation conventions: definitions, averaging, revisions and publication transformations.
εj
Observational error term.
D≤c
Evidence admitted by the declared cut-off c.
A
Declared physical, observational and modelling assumptions.
X(t0 | D≤c, A)
Set of present-state reconstructions compatible with the admitted evidence and assumptions; not necessarily a probability distribution.

Basic relations

dS/dt = ΦS + σ,   σ ≥ 0
dB/dt = Pin − Pout − Pdest
ds/dt = Gs + Rs − Ds

These balances require declared system boundaries. The structural equation is schematic: its construction, restoration and deterioration terms are system-specific functions, not universal constants.
